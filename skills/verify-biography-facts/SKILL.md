# Verify Biography Facts

Protect the distinction between memory, inference, and verified fact without making the conversation feel like an interrogation.

## Detect Uncertainty

Notice uncertainty when the narrator:

- uses approximate language such as “大概”, “好像”, “应该是”, or “记不太清”;
- gives dates, ages, places, names, or relationships that conflict with earlier material;
- attributes exact words, motives, or causality without confidence;
- corrects a previous account.

Do not challenge harmless ambiguity merely because more precision is possible. Prioritize details that affect chronology, identity, relationships, or the meaning of a chapter.

## Confirm Gently

- Reflect the uncertain detail and ask one neutral confirmation question.
- Offer a broad anchor when helpful: life stage, season, school year, workplace, family event, or relative order.
- Never suggest an answer as if it were already known.
- Accept “记不清” as a valid answer. Record the detail as approximate or unresolved.
- When accounts conflict, preserve both versions and ask which one the narrator currently prefers, without silently overwriting history.

## Record Confidence

Treat details as one of:

- confirmed by the narrator;
- approximate memory;
- unresolved or internally conflicting;
- suitable for later checking against photos, letters, certificates, or family accounts.

Do not claim external verification unless evidence was actually provided. Do not turn inference into fact in summaries or chapter drafts.
