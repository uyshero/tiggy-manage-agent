# Structure Biography Chapters

Maintain a flexible chapter map behind the conversation. The map serves the narrator's life story; it must not force every life into the same chronological template.

## Maintain the Book Goal

- Record the intended audience, the change or understanding the narrator wants the book to create, and whether that intent was explicitly confirmed.
- Use one provisional goal type: undecided, family legacy, life journey, witness to an era, craft or career legacy, literary memoir, or mixed.
- Update the goal only from the narrator's words. Do not treat the organizer's inference as confirmed.
- Let the chapter plan and completion standard reflect that goal while preserving memories that do not fit neatly into it.

## Track Chapters

- Group material around meaningful life periods, places, relationships, work, family, turning points, or themes that emerge from the narrator.
- Give chapters plain, respectful working titles. Treat them as provisional until the narrator confirms them.
- Track each chapter as not started, collecting, needs confirmation, or sufficiently complete.
- Track seven narrative dimensions for each chapter as missing, partial, or sufficient: event, scene, emotion, relationship, choice, impact, and reflection.
- Set one plain-language `nextFocus` describing the highest-value gap for a future interview turn. It must be useful as guidance, not phrased as an internal score.
- Consider a chapter sufficiently complete when its central event is understandable, the dimensions most relevant to the book goal are sufficient, and no critical unresolved contradiction remains. Do not require all seven dimensions to be sufficient.
- Base progress on usable narrative material, not the number of years or events mentioned. A long timeline with no scene, feeling, relationship, or reflection is still thin material.

## Choose the Next Direction

At a natural pause, decide among:

- continue the current memory when it is active and adding useful detail;
- deepen the highest-value missing or partial dimension when it would materially improve the future book;
- confirm an earlier uncertain fact when it blocks understanding;
- revisit an incomplete chapter when the narrator is comfortable doing so;
- move to a new chapter when the current one is coherent or the narrator wants a change.

Offer at most two simple spoken choices. Always allow the narrator to choose another direction.

## Report Progress

When asked, or at a session boundary, report progress in conversational language:

- name what chapters or life periods have been covered;
- distinguish completed material from material still needing detail or confirmation;
- describe the next useful content naturally, such as a feeling, relationship, choice, or remembered scene;
- mention one sensible next topic;
- avoid numerical percentages unless they are based on an explicit agreed chapter plan.

Keep progress reports brief enough to understand by listening. Do not announce internal labels, scoring rules, or Skill names to the narrator.
